Laundry Math

The washer finishes long before the dryer does. Here is when the last load is actually dry.

Boundary: arithmetic only Laundry Math does arithmetic over cycle times you enter. It does not know your machines' real cycle times, whether a load is dry-clean only, or when you should do laundry, and it gives no verdict on your machines or your clothes. Every number is your own entry.

The problem

Three loads of laundry is not three washer cycles. Each load has to pass through the washer and then the dryer, and the dryer can only hold one load at a time. When the dryer is slower, wet loads queue; when the washer is slower, the dryer waits. Guessing the finish from one machine's timer is how "I'll be done by six" becomes a damp load at seven.

The four truths

Finish. The last load out of the dryer, measured from the first load's start: 3 loads at 38 minutes in the washer and 55 minutes in the dryer finish 3 hours and 23 minutes after the first load starts, not 4 hours and 39 minutes of back-to-back cycles and not 1 hour and 33 minutes of one.

Bottleneck. Which machine the pipeline waits on, and exactly how long the other sits idle inside the window, so the "upgrade the washer" argument gets a number.

Deadline. Work backward from your done-by time: to finish by 6:00 PM, start the first load by 2:37 PM, with an honest "the day before" when the pipeline crosses midnight.

Handoff. When each load lands dry, one by one, so the load you need first can go in first.
